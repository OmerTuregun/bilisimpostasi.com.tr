Since its founding, Amazon has operated as an online bookseller, establishing deep connections with libraries and the publishing industry. However, recent reports reveal the company is not hesitant to sever ties with this legacy.

Data sources required to train artificial intelligence models are becoming increasingly scarce. With virtually all publicly accessible content already utilized, companies have begun searching for less accessible and more valuable resources. Rare books have emerged as an ideal source for training large language models (LLMs).

Amazon's practice of destroying rare books to convert them into AI training data highlights the tension between technological advancement and cultural preservation. Critical questions are emerging: Is it ethical to destroy cultural heritage in the name of AI development? Should rare and unique books be valued only as data points?

This development demonstrates how far data hunger in the technology sector can reach. Authors, librarians, and cultural advocates are growing concerned about the implications. While seemingly efficient at first glance, this approach could threaten cultural diversity and access to information in the long term.

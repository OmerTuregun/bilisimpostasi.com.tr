The sources used to train artificial intelligence models have become increasingly controversial in recent times. A recent discovery involving Amazon's practices has shed light on just how far the company is willing to go in pursuit of this goal.

Uncovered through AirTag tracking, the situation reveals that Amazon has been using rare and valuable books to develop its AI models, only to destroy them afterward. This practice raises several critical questions:

**Data Source Acquisition Methods**: Libraries containing diverse and high-quality books play a crucial role in training large language models and other AI systems. The methods Amazon uses to obtain these sources and whether it adheres to ethical standards throughout this process is a significant concern.

**Intellectual Property and Copyright**: Rare books typically hold considerable value and are protected by copyright. Using such materials for AI training raises various legal and ethical questions that cannot be overlooked.

**Sustainability and Responsibility**: The disposal of valuable cultural assets extends beyond a technical issue—it also carries profound implications for the preservation of cultural heritage.

This incident serves as a reminder that technology companies must adopt more transparent and responsible practices in their AI development processes.

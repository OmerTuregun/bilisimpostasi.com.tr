The Electronic Border Foundation (EFF) warned that the increasing hype on artificial intelligence threatens copyright laws. EFF gives a message to courts: do not change the existing copyright infrastructure based on technological developments.

The main points of the discussion are:

- **AI training data problem**: Legal uncertainty is experienced in the use of copyrighted content in the training of artificial intelligence systems
-**: EFF indicates that the restrictive laws that can be brought by hype can damage creative technologies
- **Is applicable laws sufficient**: The question of whether the copyright laws have already arranged and edit the use of AI within the framework of fair use principles remain uncertain
- ** Protection of human creators**: How to solve the question of how the machines can learn without violating the rights of human creators Tags

The basic argument of EFF is that creatives should also care about copyright protection as innovations that technological developments offer. However, rather than removing rush and comprehensive laws to ensure this balance, it argues that the existing legal framework should be carefully implemented.

Perhaps the main difficulty is that the artificial intelligence technology adapts the legal system against the speed of rapid development, the balanic protection itself.

Privacy and oversight remain at the center of conflicts between technology companies and regulatory authorities. Apple's App Tracking Transparency (ATT) feature exemplifies this ongoing tension.

Launched with iOS 14.5 in 2021, ATT was designed to give users control over app-based tracking across applications. While theoretically aimed at protecting users, the system has caused significant harm in practice, resulting in nearly $10 billion in losses for social media applications.

Complaints filed by Germany's Federal Cartel Office suggest that Apple designed this system to favor its own applications. For example, the design of permission request screens discourages users from allowing tracking—though this standard is not applied equally across all applications.

Since the European Union has designated Apple a "gatekeeper" under the Digital Markets Act (DMA), the company faces additional scrutiny. This designation raises critical questions about how Apple wields its dominant position within the App Store ecosystem.

This case underscores how delicate the balance remains between technology giants and regulatory bodies. Design choices made in the name of privacy can ultimately become instruments for wielding market power.

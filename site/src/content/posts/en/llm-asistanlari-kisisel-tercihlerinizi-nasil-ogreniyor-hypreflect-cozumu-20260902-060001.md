When artificial intelligence assistants are increasingly located in our daily life, the ability to adapt to our individual preferences directly affects the quality of the user experience. However, personalization is a much more complex process than clearly specified preferences, and often occurs with hidden, noisy signals.

New research published in ArXiv offers an innovative approach called **HypReflect**:

- Learning user preferences from the history of interaction, avoiding the cost of creating artificial reward models
- While existing methods are based on raw interaction data or expensive reward optimization, the new system uses hypothesis guided selfdestilation
- Detection of heterogeneous and hidden preferences, making long-term user-AI interactions more effective
- The system increases integration depth of AI assistants by providing more reliable customization performance

This research leads conversation-based assistants to better understand their users in time. This method provides accuracy while reducing data consistency and cost can significantly improve the scalability of LLM applications in the future.

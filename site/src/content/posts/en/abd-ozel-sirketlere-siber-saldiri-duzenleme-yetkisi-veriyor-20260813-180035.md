The United States is reversing a policy that has been in place for decades, which prohibited private companies from conducting offensive cyber operations. Under this historic policy shift, select private firms will now be authorized to launch "counterattacks" against cyber threats targeting them.

While this decision empowers companies to actively defend themselves against cyber threats, it carries significant risks. Security experts warn that the move could lead to uncontrolled cyber warfare and an escalation of international tensions.

As the defensive capabilities of private entities strengthen, close monitoring will be essential to determine how these companies use their new authority and what legal consequences may emerge from this precedent-setting decision.

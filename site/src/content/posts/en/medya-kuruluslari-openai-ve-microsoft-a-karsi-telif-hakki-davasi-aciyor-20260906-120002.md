News industry shows an increasing resistance to AI companies. Seattle Times and Newsday gave OpenAI and Microsoft court due to copyright breach. Both broadcasters claim that their original journalism is used without permission in the education of large language models.

These legal steps represent a trend that starts in the news sector in the remaining months:

- The opening of a previous similar case of New York Times brought the copyright concerns of media organizations
- Seattle Times and Newsday advances that their journalism studies are included in AI models without written permission
- OpenAI and Microsoft are preparing for defense strategies to protect commercial interests of news posts
- These cases bring critical questions about the legal status of AI training data sets into the agenda
- copyright vs. fair usage frame discussion concentrates on technology and legal environment

Media organizations think that AI models lose their competitive advantages by learning from news content. This situation has become an important trial stone on how content makers are protected in the AI era. The response and decision of the court has the potential to determine the legal framework of future AI applications.

This conflict between the news industry and the artificial intelligence sector can lead to similar actions of other content producers in the later period.

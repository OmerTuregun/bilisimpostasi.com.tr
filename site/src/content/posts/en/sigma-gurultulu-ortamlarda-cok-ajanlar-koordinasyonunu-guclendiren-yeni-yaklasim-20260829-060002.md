Decision making in multi-million systems offers great challenges in the real world. It is a critical problem to maintain coordination between agents especially under noise sensor data and missing observations. A new research offers an innovative method called **SIGMA** (Builded Noise-Etki-Fark Grouped Multi-Ajanlar Agregation) to address this difficulty.

Basic findings of research:

- Although the noise is independently affecting each agent, it shows that these effects in the collaborative structure are made **
- Noise-based decision effects reveal that not only individually, should be analyzed in group dynamics
- Multi-regulants aim to significantly improve coordination quality (MARL)
- Thanks to structure awareness, it makes the system resistance to observation distortion

This study represents an important step in the field of Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL). Researchers aim to build more reliable and stable collaborative systems by modeling the way of spreading between agents of noise.

This approach can revolutionize applications such as autonomous robots, distributed control systems and multiplayer video games. The success of SIGMA can improve the applicability of artificial intelligence systems that collaborate in noisy and uncertain environments.

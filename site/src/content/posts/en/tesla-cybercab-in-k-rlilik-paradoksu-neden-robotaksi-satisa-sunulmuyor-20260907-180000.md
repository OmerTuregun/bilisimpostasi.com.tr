Tesla’s Cybercab project is a critical engineering work that will shape the future of autonomous vehicle technology. But**A assumptional scenario** brings this question: if the Tesla’s robotic fleet was really profitable, why do you think to sell it to individual customers? This logic is an important observation that explains the economic dynamics of the artificial intelligence-supported transportation industry.

The logic behind the profitability issue can be explained as follows:

- If every Cybercab generates steady income as autonomous, Tesla’s most rational moves would be to operate the entire fleet itself
- Retail sales means giving up this income stream and reduces the company’s long-term profit
- The actual high value of autonomous vehicles, **milyon does not sell the device, comes from providing sustainable service
- In addition, the responsibility, insurance and operational risks that individual owners may encounter also add high costs to the system

Such business model selections are often seen in the technology industry. Keeping them under control really high value assets can be more advantageous than selling them out. Tesla’s Cybercab strategy requires not only technical success, but also the test of economic rationality. If you prefer not on sale, this is in its own business,**The proof of the real profitability of the system.

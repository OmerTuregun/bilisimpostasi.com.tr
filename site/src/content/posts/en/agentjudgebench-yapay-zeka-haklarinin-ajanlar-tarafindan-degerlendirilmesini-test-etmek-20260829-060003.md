Large language models (LLM) are increasingly mastered to evaluate the performance of artificial intelligence systems. However, there are little information about how reliable this "LLM-as-a-judge" approach is especially in complex workflows. Here a new research appeared to fill this gap.

AgentJudgeBench developed by researchers was designed to systematically examine the reliability of LLM judges in evaluation of tool call (tool-calling) systems. This set of criteria offers:

- Extensive data set consisting of 3,808 test sample
- Reviews at multiple difficulty levels
- Measurements focused on work flow DAG structures
- Different structured tasks from open-end text assessment
- scenarios in real world in dependency-oriented workflows

Specifically remarkable point is that existing LLM-as-a-judge studies are mainly focused on simple text comparison or preference assessment. AgentJudgeBench covers more complex scenarios where artificial intelligence tools should be called in multiple steps.

This comparison will help AI developers and researchers understand how LLM judges perform with actual production ready-made agent systems. After all, the reliable evaluation of tool call systems is critical to developing smarter and safer AI applications.

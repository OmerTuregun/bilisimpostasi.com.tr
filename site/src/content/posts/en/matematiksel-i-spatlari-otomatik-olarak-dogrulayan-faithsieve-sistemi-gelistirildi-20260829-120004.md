Large language models can now produce complex, multi-step mathematical evidence but it continues to be an important problem to reliably determine the accuracy of these evidence and localize logical errors. A new system called FaithSieve uses official evidence verifyors to solve this difficulty.

Current assessment methods are basically facing these limitations:

- The model-based natural language is dependent on judgments and the local reasoning gaps are missing
- I can't determine what steps the errors are formed
- No precise verification is available

FaithSieve addresses this problem by using formalorem evidence such as Lean. The system can automatically control every logical step by converting evidence written in natural language into official statements. Thanks to this approach, evidence can be determined not only correctly or incorrect, but at which point the error is made.

The research represents an important step to improve the quality of mathematical work produced by artificial intelligence. Formal verification is a critical mechanism that provides reliability in academic studies and mathematical research. Systems such as FaithSieve can make it possible to use language models in a more responsible way and prevent the spread of false evidence.
